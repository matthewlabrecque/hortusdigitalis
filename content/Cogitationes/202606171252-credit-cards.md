### Why credit cards?
When they are used correctly, credit cards can be a great way to earn additional rewards on your purchases while also providing purchase protection and other benefits (such as no foreign transaction fees). The downside of them however is that it can be very easy to fall into the credit card trap of going into debt since you don't have to pay them off all at once. This is why it's important to always pay your credit cards off in full at the end of the month.
### What are "Cents-per-Mile"?
> [!info]
> (Information is sourced from [this Bankrate article](https://www.bankrate.com/credit-cards/travel/points-and-miles-valuations/))
> The value of airline and hotel rewards vary dramatically between rewards programs and booking details, ranging in value from roughly 0.4 cents per point or mile to 3 cents per point or mile. Many general travel credit cards earn rewards that can be redeemed at one value for travel with the issuer, usually 1.0 cent per point, or transferred to an airline or hotel program and redeemed at another (potentially higher) value.
> 
> To see how much value you can get out of your rewards on an individual flight or hotel stay, divide the cost of a booking in cash by its cost in points or miles.
> 
> For example, a flight that costs \$139 or 21,500 points will offer about 0.6 cents per point in value (\$139 / 21,500 points = ~\$0.006). If the same flight costs \$250, you’d get about 1.2 cents per point in value (\$250 / 21,500 points = ~\$0.012).
> 
> Keep in mind that the value you can get out of your rewards will depend not only on the program but also the details of your booking. Some airline and hotel rewards carry a better point value than others on average, but your experience may vary based on when and where you travel, as well as whether you opt for a luxury or budget fare class or hotel tier.

- I don't even keep this card in my wallet anymore since it's only for rent
- **Rewards:**
	- 1 point per dollar on rent through BILT Pay
	- 1 point per dollar on everyday spend
